I trained as a computational physicist. Now I work on how scientific software performs: on CPUs, on GPUs, and in the hands of AI agents.

about

I am a Research Software Engineer at University of Cambridge (Research Computing Services), where I develop high-performance software for research. I hold a PhD in theoretical chemistry from TU Dresden (supervised by Prof. Thomas Heine) and was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie ITN fellow (2exciting).

My experience spans academia and industry: I contributed to commercial quantum chemistry software at Software for Chemistry & Materials (SCM) and collaborated within DFG CRC 1415 and HZDR-CASUS. My interdisciplinary and international experience includes research in chemistry, materials science, and physics across Germany, the Netherlands, France (CNRS‑LNCMI), and China (BUAA - supervised by Prof. Naihua Miao and Chinese Academy of Sciences-IEE).

Research interests

During my doctoral research, I focused on:

  • Structural and opto-electronic properties of twisted transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDCs) heterostructures
  • Multiscale modelling, from classical force fields to electronic-structure methods
  • Development and implementation of semi-empirical quantum-chemistry methods
current work focus
  • HPC performance engineering: benchmarking, profiling, optimising and parallelising research software on CPUs and GPUs, backed by CI.
  • Evaluating and deploying AI coding agents for science: a reproducible benchmark comparing locally served LLMs on scientific correctness and reliability, and layered guardrails for agents on shared HPC clusters, tested with an A/B harness.
research software

Selected research software I wrote or contributed to during my PhD.

AMS/DFTB: Spin-Orbit Coupling in SCC-DFTB/GFN-xTBCommercial code Role: Contributor — implemented self-consistent spin-orbit coupling in AMS/DFTB end to end, released in AMS2025.
  • SCC-DFTB and GFN-xTB; molecules and periodic systems; MPI-parallel; from input to SOC band structures and DOS in the AMS GUI.
  • Software for Chemistry & Materials B.V. (SCM)
  • Relevant publication: W. Li, P. Philipsen, T. Brumme, T. Heine. Advanced Quantum Technologies 9(4), e01001 (2026)
  • ml_soc_xtb: A physics-constrained ML protocol that learns environment-dependent SOC parameters inside the Hamiltonian from DFT
    • The goal is SOC parameters for AMS/DFTB. AMS can supply DFT references natively; the open-source GPAW + DFTB+ stack keeps the protocol reproducible outside AMS.
twistHS Builds commensurate moiré supercells for twisted van der Waals bilayers, with moiré analysis and an MLIP relaxation workflow. Role: Sole developer. Download
  • Coincidence-lattice search over Hermite-normal-form sublattices that preserves the moiré wave vectors, up to higher order, rather than strain alone; a polar decomposition separates each layer's rotation from its strain.
  • Vectorized construction builds a 1.3M-atom supercell in 0.15 s (~4,000× faster); optional JAX backend for GPU.
  • MLIP workflow: MACE-MP-0 relaxation and fine-tuning; inexpensive FF labels test whether small cells capture moiré local environments, and pre-registered criteria set a go/no-go decision and DFT budget before any DFT is spent.
  • Relevant publication: W. Li, T. Brumme, T. Heine. npj 2D Mater Appl 8, 43 (2024)
